# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- Real progress on focus: brass and "any country" are gone. It is a food exporter now, selling to named buyer types in named countries, and that is a real cut.
- B2B-only is a useful constraint. The buyer experience can be mapped end to end: the sample, the spec sheet, the order, the shipment, the paperwork and the re-order.
- Spices and groundnuts are things where quality shows up in details the buyer checks: moisture, purity, aflatoxin, pesticide residue, grind consistency and packing. A business that gets those details right every time has a way to stand out besides price.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Still no "one thing".** Five product families times five countries is 25 businesses, each with different regulations, shelf lives and buyers. Ready-to-eat meals and namkeen need different process control from whole spices. If the one-sentence pitch is "Indian food, to five countries", buyers hear "another trader".
2. **No owned quality.** If this is a merchant exporter reselling other people's output, the parts nobody sees (lot testing, packing, fumigation, documents) belong to suppliers. One rejected container at the destination port ends the relationship with that buyer, and it is the seam this lens worries about most.
3. **The buyer experience is not designed.** Nobody has described what an importer in Dubai or Ho Chi Minh City gets in their first week: how fast samples arrive, what the certificate pack looks like, who answers when a shipment is late. Without that, the only thing left to offer is price.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Say it in one sentence: which product, to which buyer, in which country, and why you rather than the trader next door?
- Will you manufacture or process anything yourself, or source and resell? Who signs off on each lot before it ships?
- What does a new buyer receive between first contact and second order? Walk me through it step by step.
- Which certifications do you hold today, and which does your first market require? (/founder-ops)
- What are the price, margin and order size on one container? (/founder-cfo, open)
- Who already supplies these buyers, and what do they complain about? (/founder-competitors, open)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Cut to one product line and one country for the first 6-12 months.** My suggestion is whole and ground spices to the UAE. Spices are shelf-stable and their quality can be tested by lot, so craft can be shown. The UAE is a re-export hub with an established trade in Indian food, which makes it a likely place to learn. *This choice is unverified. /founder-competitors must check how crowded it is, and the CFO must check margins.* If those checks show a less crowded country/product pair, take that pair instead.
2. **Own quality control on every shipment,** with an independent lab report per lot and a written spec, even if production is outsourced.
3. **Write the buyer journey down** from first enquiry to re-order, with one named owner at each handoff.
4. **Delay ready-to-eat meals, namkeen and the other three countries** until the first pair produces repeat orders.
