# Monopoly lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- The origin story makes sense. Gujarat is a known source for groundnuts and spices, and Jamnagar for brass parts. Being near the supply could be a real starting point if the founder actually has access to it, which the brief does not say.
- Export trading can start small. One category, one destination and one importer is a market small enough to win first, if the founder chooses one.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Commodity competition with no edge.** Spices, groundnuts and generic FMCG goods are sold by many exporters to the same importers. When a buyer cannot tell sellers apart, they compare on price and margins stay thin. Nothing in the brief is ten times better than the alternatives, and there is no network effect, scale advantage or brand yet.
2. **No distribution plan.** The founder has not said who the buyers are, which countries they are in, or how the first overseas customers will be found. Without a way to reach buyers, there is no business yet, only a product list. The cost of winning each buyer is unknown.
3. **No focus, so no small market to own.** Five unrelated categories need different certifications, quality standards and buyers. Brass parts are an engineering supply chain, while food is a regulated food supply chain. Spread across all five, the business will lead in none of them. In month six, any other Gujarat merchant exporter could copy it.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which single category and which single destination country come first, and why that pair?
- What do you know about this trade that most exporters do not? That could be a supplier, a quality grade, a buyer relationship, or a compliance problem others keep failing.
- Do you manufacture, or source and resell? If you resell, what stops your supplier from selling to your buyer directly?
- How do your first 10 buyers find you, and what does each one cost to win? (Open question: /founder-competitors and /founder-consumer.)
- What margin is left per container after sourcing, freight and certification? (Open question: /founder-cfo. No numbers have been given.)
- What makes this business harder to copy in year ten than it is in year one?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable: the vote is PASS. I would reconsider if the founder came back with:
