# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- Real progress since round 1: brass and "other FMCG" are gone, "any country" became five, and the buyer is now named (importers, wholesalers, retail chains). That is the first step toward a market small enough to own.
- Origin edge is possible. Gujarat is a known growing and trading region for spices such as cumin, and for groundnuts. Being close to the source could become know-how others lack: grading, crop timing, knowing which lots are good.
- B2B buyers place repeat orders. One importer who trusts the supply can be worth years of volume, so a small beachhead can still matter.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No small market to dominate.** Five product groups across five countries is still 25 crowded markets, each already served by established Indian exporters and by other origin countries. A newcomer wins none of them by default. Without a beachhead, the only lever left is price, and competing on price destroys margin.
2. **No secret and no durability.** Nothing here is ten times better: no proprietary process, brand, network effect or scale yet. If it is a merchant exporter reselling bought stock, a copycat with an IEC and the same suppliers can match it in month six. Why will this still hold a position in ten years?
3. **Distribution is unanswered.** How the first overseas buyers are found is still blank. With no channel, no cost per buyer and no proof of quality, it is a product list, not a business.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- If you could sell one product to one buyer type in one country, which would it be, and why would they switch to you?
- What do you know about this supply chain that other Gujarat exporters do not?
- Do you make, process, grade or pack anything yourself, or only resell?
- Who are your first ten named buyers, and how will you reach them? What does each cost to win? (/founder-consumer; cost per buyer in /founder-cfo)
- Who already sells this product to that buyer, at what price, and what do buyers complain about? (/founder-competitors)
- Which certifications and supplier relationships do you hold today?
- What margin is left after freight, duties and payment terms? (/founder-cfo)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
The vote is PASS, but it would become FUND IF:
1. The founder picks **one product, one country, one buyer type**. My suggested beachhead is a Gujarat-origin product such as cumin or groundnuts, where being close to the source is most likely to count, sold to a narrow buyer group. That might be specialist importers in one country rather than all of UAE's crowded general trade. Needs verification by /founder-competitors, which should check how crowded each of the five countries is for that product.
2. The founder names a defensible edge: verified grading or quality control, exclusive farm or processor access, or a cleaning/packing step that others do not offer.
3. There is a written plan to land the first 10 buyers, with a cost for each, and at least one sample order or letter of intent.
