# Product lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- The base is credible. Gujarat is a known origin for groundnuts and spices, and Jamnagar for brass parts, so a single focused line could be sourced close to home.
- Export trading is a well-understood model. It does not need a new behaviour from buyers, only a better experience than the exporters they already use.
- It is early enough to cut hard before money is spent.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No focus.** Spices, groundnuts, brass parts, processed food "and other FMCG" are five businesses with different buyers, certifications, quality checks and shipping. As stated, this is a catalogue, not a product. Nothing here can be done brilliantly because nothing has been chosen.
2. **No customer, so no experience to design.** We do not know the country or the buyer type (importer, distributor, retailer, online), so we cannot describe the moment an overseas buyer opens a container and judges it. Without that, quality, packing, documents and follow-up have no standard to meet.
3. **Seams the founder does not own.** If this is a merchant exporter reselling from farmers and factories, the parts the buyer cares most about (consistency of grade, contamination, finish on brass parts, on-time shipping) sit with other people. One bad lot or a rejected shipment is the product, whatever the website says.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence: which one product, to which buyer, in which country?
- Describe the first shipment from the buyer's side: what arrives, what do they check, what makes them reorder?
- Do you make anything, or source and resell? Who controls quality at each handover, and how?
- Which certifications and licences do you hold today?
- What would you cut first, and why that one?
- Open questions for later steps: who already supplies that buyer and at what price (/founder-competitors); whether buyers would switch and what would make them (/founder-consumer); order size, margin, payment terms and cash tied up per shipment (/founder-cfo).

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable as a PASS. This vote would move to FUND IF if the founder comes back with: one category, one buyer type, one target country; a one-sentence pitch; a named way to control quality at every handover; and a written description of the buyer's first-shipment experience that the operation is built backwards from.
